If you run a small or mid-size manufacturing company and you're evaluating SAP, you've probably already felt the friction: a sales call before you see a price, a multi-month implementation quote before you've even piloted the software, and a feature set built for a much bigger company than yours. The good news is that SAP alternatives for manufacturing have matured a lot in the last five years. NetSuite, Epicor, Odoo, Fulcrum, MRPeasy, and newer API-first platforms like Carbon now cover most of what SMB manufacturers actually need, often at a fraction of the cost and time.

This is not a hit piece on SAP — Business One and S/4HANA Public Cloud are real products with real strengths, and they're covered in more depth in our [SAP Business One assessment](/learn/sap-business-one-for-small-manufacturers) and our breakdown of [what SAP actually costs a 50-person machine shop](/learn/what-sap-costs-a-50-person-machine-shop). This article is about what to switch to, or start with instead, if you've decided SAP isn't the right fit for a shop your size.

## What "SMB manufacturer" actually needs from an ERP

Before comparing tools, it helps to define the job. A discrete manufacturer with 10-250 employees typically needs:

- **Multi-level BOM and routing management** that reflects how the part is actually built, not just a parts list.
- **MRP** that nets demand against on-hand, on-order, and lead times, and turns that into purchase and work order suggestions.
- **Shop floor execution** — traveler or work-order tracking, labor and machine time capture, and status visibility without a spreadsheet relay race.
- **Inventory accuracy** across raw, WIP, and finished goods, ideally with lot/serial traceability if you sell into regulated industries.
- **Job or product costing** that ties actual labor, material, and overhead back to a specific job so you know your real margin, not just your quoted margin.
- **Purchasing and sales order management** that talks to the same data as production, so a late PO shows up as a schedule risk automatically.
- Increasingly, **an API** — to connect a CAD tool, a quoting form, a customer portal, or an AI agent — without waiting on a systems integrator.

Legacy ERP was built assuming a company would hire an implementation partner to configure all of this by hand. The alternatives below take different bets on how much of that should be self-serve.

## The contenders

### NetSuite

NetSuite is Oracle's cloud ERP and the closest thing to an "SAP alternative" that finance and ops leaders will already recognize by name. Its manufacturing edition adds work orders, basic routing, and assembly builds on top of NetSuite's strong core financials — which is genuinely one of the best in this list if accounting and multi-entity/multi-currency consolidation is your top priority. The tradeoffs: manufacturing depth is thinner than a shop-floor-native system (no real finite scheduling or MES out of the box), pricing is quote-based and typically requires an annual contract plus implementation partner, and heavy customization runs through SuiteScript, which usually means hiring a NetSuite consultant.

**Best for:** manufacturers where multi-entity finance and consolidation matter as much as production control.

### Epicor Kinetic

Epicor Kinetic is a genuine manufacturing ERP, not a finance system with manufacturing bolted on. It covers MRP, scheduling, quality, and a configurable BOM engine that has served discrete manufacturers for decades (Epicor's lineage goes back to Vantage and Vista). Kinetic's strength is breadth and industry-specific configuration; the tradeoff is a licensing and implementation model that still looks a lot like traditional enterprise ERP — quote-based pricing, a partner-led rollout, and a learning curve that reflects nearly 30 years of accumulated functionality.

**Best for:** established manufacturers that want deep, industry-tuned manufacturing functionality and have the budget and timeline for a traditional implementation.

### Odoo

Odoo is the open-source generalist: a suite of dozens of apps (CRM, sales, inventory, manufacturing, accounting) on a shared PostgreSQL backend, with a free Community edition and a paid Enterprise tier. For a small manufacturer that also needs CRM, e-commerce, or HR, the appeal of one connected suite is real, and Community is genuinely free to self-host. The catch for manufacturing specifically: Odoo's MRP module does infinite-capacity scheduling out of the box (finite scheduling needs frePPLe or a third-party app), and Quality, PLM, and Shop Floor are Enterprise-only add-ons, which changes the cost math once you need them. We cover this in detail in our [Odoo alternatives for manufacturers](/learn/best-odoo-alternatives-for-manufacturers) piece.

**Best for:** small manufacturers who want a free, self-hostable, horizontal business suite and can live without finite scheduling and native QMS.

### Fulcrum

Fulcrum is a modern, well-built MES/ERP hybrid aimed squarely at job shops and machine shops, with real strengths in scheduling visualization, a public REST API, and even an in-app AI assistant. It's one of the more thoughtfully designed tools on this list for shop-floor teams. The tradeoff is scope: Fulcrum is strongest on production and scheduling and leans on integrations for deeper accounting, so a shop that wants native, audited financials in the same system may still need a connected accounting package.

**Best for:** job shops that prioritize scheduling and shop-floor usability and are comfortable integrating accounting separately.

### MRPeasy

MRPeasy is a lightweight, affordably priced cloud MRP/ERP built for small manufacturers — typically under 50 employees — who've outgrown spreadsheets but don't want enterprise complexity. It covers BOM, MRP, basic scheduling, and inventory well for its size, with transparent per-user pricing. The honest limitation is ceiling: it's not built for complex multi-level BOMs at scale, deep quality management, or regulated/defense manufacturing, and most integrations are basic.

**Best for:** very small manufacturers who need to get off spreadsheets fast and don't have complex compliance requirements.

### Carbon

Carbon is an API-first, source-available operating system for manufacturing — ERP, MRP, MES, and QMS on one Postgres data model, rather than separate modules stitched together. Every table and capability is reachable over a documented REST API (rest.carbon.ms) and a hosted MCP server, so it's straightforward to connect CAD, a customer portal, or an AI agent like Claude or Cursor to live production data without a systems integrator. It includes native accounting and job costing (not a bolt-on), a parametric configurator for BOM/routing/price resolution, and a native 3D/STEP viewer on the shop floor. You can run it as managed SaaS or self-host it — including GovCloud/ITAR-capable Enterprise deployments — and because the full source is on GitHub, you're never locked into a vendor's release schedule. Pricing is published, not sales-gated: Starter is $40/user/month, Business is $100/user/month (5-user minimum) with API/webhooks and audit logging included, and there's a 30-day free trial with no sales call required.

**Best for:** manufacturers that want ERP+MES+QMS in one system, need to extend it programmatically, or want the option to self-host — without committing to a quote-based sales cycle first.

## Why open, API-first, and self-hostable matters more than it used to

Two things have changed since most legacy ERP was designed. First, manufacturers increasingly want to connect their CAD tool, their quoting form, their customer portal, and now AI agents directly to production data — which only works cleanly if the ERP exposes a real API over its *entire* data model, not a curated subset of endpoints. Second, defense, aerospace, and medical manufacturers face compliance requirements (ITAR, CMMC, NIST 800-171) that are dramatically simpler to satisfy when you can self-host the system in your own environment rather than trusting a vendor's shared cloud. Carbon was built around both of these realities: the API and MCP server cover every table, and self-hosting is a supported deployment path, not an afterthought.

Published, no-sales-call pricing is the third piece. If you can't get a real number without a discovery call, you can't budget an ERP switch the way you'd budget any other software purchase — and that opacity is exactly what pushes many SMB manufacturers away from SAP and its peers in the first place.

## How to actually choose

Start by ranking your non-negotiables: if it's multi-entity financial consolidation, look hard at NetSuite. If it's deep, industry-specific manufacturing configuration and you have the budget and time for a traditional rollout, Epicor Kinetic is worth the process. If you want a free, horizontal suite and can live without finite scheduling and native QMS, Odoo is a reasonable bet — read our [honest Odoo review](/learn/best-odoo-alternatives-for-manufacturers) before committing. If you're a job shop that mainly needs scheduling and shop-floor visibility, try Fulcrum. If you're very small and just need to escape spreadsheets, MRPeasy is the fastest, cheapest path. And if you want ERP, MES, and QMS unified on one model with a real API, the option to self-host, and pricing you can see before you talk to anyone, put Carbon on your shortlist and see our [full comparison against SAP Business One](/compare/sap-business-one).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the cheapest SAP alternative for a small manufacturer?

For very small shops, MRPeasy and Odoo Community are the lowest-cost entry points. For a company that needs unified ERP+MES+QMS with native job costing, Carbon's Starter plan at $40/user/month is typically far below SAP Business One's total cost once implementation and add-on modules are included — see the full breakdown in [what SAP actually costs a 50-person machine shop](/learn/what-sap-costs-a-50-person-machine-shop).

### Do any of these alternatives support ITAR or CMMC compliance?

Carbon is audit-ready for CMMC/NIST 800-171 — the RBAC, audit-logging, and encryption controls the standard requires are implemented in the platform — and supports ITAR-capable self-hosted or GovCloud deployments on its Enterprise plan. Most of the other tools in this list are single-tenant cloud SaaS without a supported self-host path, which makes ITAR data residency requirements harder to satisfy.

### Can I try any of these before committing to an implementation?

Odoo Community and Carbon both offer genuine self-serve access — Odoo by self-hosting the free edition, Carbon via a 30-day free trial at [app.carbon.ms](https://app.carbon.ms) with no sales call required. NetSuite, Epicor, and Fulcrum typically require a sales conversation and quote before you can evaluate the product hands-on.

### Is Odoo or Carbon a better fit for a manufacturer specifically?

Odoo is a horizontal business suite where manufacturing is one app among dozens, which is great if you also need CRM or e-commerce natively. Carbon is manufacturing-native from the data model up — finite scheduling, real QMS, and job costing are built in rather than added on, which tends to matter more as production complexity grows.

### How long does implementation typically take?

Traditional ERP implementations (NetSuite, Epicor, SAP) commonly run 4-12 months for an SMB manufacturer once you include a systems integrator. Lighter tools like MRPeasy can be running in weeks. Carbon implementations typically take about a month, since the data model is unified and there's no module-by-module configuration to reconcile.
