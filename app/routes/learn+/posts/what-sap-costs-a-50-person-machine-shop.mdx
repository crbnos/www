SAP doesn't publish a price list for SAP Business One, and that opacity is itself the most important fact for a small manufacturer to know before starting an evaluation. Every quote — licenses, implementation, and add-ons like Beas Manufacturing or Produmex — runs through a value-added reseller (VAR), and the total varies by region, reseller, and negotiating leverage. Below is an illustrative, clearly-labeled cost model for a 50-person machine shop, built from the cost categories every B1 buyer actually encounters, so you can ask your VAR sharper questions instead of walking in blind.

**Everything dollar figure below is an example for illustration, not a quote from SAP or a Carbon-verified number.** Treat it as a framework for the questions to ask, not a bid.

## Why SAP Business One pricing is opaque by design

SAP Business One is sold exclusively through certified VARs — SAP doesn't sell it direct, and it doesn't list per-user pricing on its website. This isn't unusual for enterprise software, but it has real consequences for a small manufacturer trying to budget:

- You can't comparison-shop without multiple sales calls, each requiring a discovery meeting before you see a number.
- The same license can be quoted differently by two VARs in the same city, based on their margin structure and the services they bundle in.
- Add-ons like Beas Manufacturing and Produmex are licensed and quoted separately, by a different vendor than the one selling you B1 itself — so your "SAP quote" is really at least two vendors' quotes stitched together.
- Maintenance and support terms (commonly quoted as an annual percentage of license value, a structure typical across enterprise software) are easy to skim past during a sales conversation and expensive to discover later.

None of this makes SAP Business One a bad product — it makes it a product whose true cost only becomes visible once you've assembled every line item yourself. That's the exercise below.

## Building the cost model: what actually gets billed

A realistic SAP Business One deployment for a 50-person machine shop has five cost categories:

1. **User licenses** — typically split between full transactional users (who create orders, post journal entries, run MRP) and limited/logistics users (who mostly view or perform narrow tasks like time entry). Commonly reported per-user costs across SAP B1 deployments range roughly **$95–$250 per user per month**, depending on license tier, region, and whether it's cloud-hosted or on-premise.
2. **VAR implementation services** — discovery, configuration, data migration, and training, billed by the VAR. For a first ERP implementation with real manufacturing requirements, this commonly runs as a multiple of the first year's license cost rather than a flat fee.
3. **Manufacturing add-ons** — Beas Manufacturing for shop-floor execution and finite scheduling, Produmex for warehouse execution, each with its own license fee on top of B1 itself.
4. **Annual maintenance/support** — enterprise software maintenance contracts are commonly priced around 18–22% of license value per year industry-wide; SAP ecosystem products are typically in that range.
5. **Ongoing customization and change requests** — the VAR relationship doesn't end at go-live. Report changes, new users, workflow tweaks, and version upgrades typically continue as billable VAR time.

Two things to notice. First, the manufacturing add-ons — the part of the system a 50-person machine shop actually needs to run the floor — aren't in SAP's own license line at all; they're a second (and sometimes third) vendor relationship layered on top. Second, the implementation cost is a range wide enough to swing the entire first-year number by tens of thousands of dollars, because it's set by your specific VAR's rate card, not a published price.

## The costs that show up after go-live

The year-1 and steady-state numbers above cover the predictable line items. In practice, a handful of costs tend to surface only after implementation, and they're worth budgeting for even though no VAR quote will show them up front:

- **Change requests.** Adding a new report, a new approval step, or a new field to a form is rarely free once you're past the initial statement of work — it's billed as VAR consulting time, at whatever hourly rate is in your contract.
- **New-user onboarding.** Every seat you add later comes at the same per-user rate as your original license, plus whatever training the VAR bills for getting that person productive on the classic interface.
- **Version upgrades.** When SAP ships a new B1 release, Beas and Produmex each need to be recertified against it by their own vendors before you can safely upgrade — which sometimes means waiting on a third party's timeline, or paying for expedited compatibility work.
- **Add-on license true-ups.** If your headcount on the shop floor grows, Beas and Produmex licensing typically grows with it, on a separate renewal cycle from your core B1 contract.
- **Multi-vendor support triage.** When something breaks, the first troubleshooting step is often figuring out whether the issue is in B1, Beas, Produmex, or the integration between them — and coordinating three support desks costs time even when none of them charge for the ticket itself.

None of these costs are unique to SAP; any multi-vendor enterprise stack accumulates them. The point is that a single license-and-implementation quote — the number most shops budget against — rarely represents the full run-rate two or three years in.

## Questions to ask your VAR before you sign

If you're already deep in a SAP Business One evaluation, these are the questions that turn an opaque quote into a comparable one:

- What is the per-user license cost, broken down by license tier (full vs. limited), and is it perpetual or subscription?
- Is Beas Manufacturing or Produmex required for our use case, and what is that license cost — quoted by whom, and under what contract?
- What is the annual maintenance percentage, and does it increase over time?
- What is included in the implementation quote, and what's billed separately as a change request afterward?
- Who provides support after go-live — the VAR, SAP directly, or the add-on vendor — and what are the response-time commitments?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does SAP Business One cost per user?

SAP doesn't publish per-user pricing; it's quoted through a VAR and commonly reported in the range of $95–$250 per user per month depending on license tier, region, and deployment model. Treat any published range, including this one, as a starting point for VAR conversations, not a quote.

### Does the SAP Business One price include manufacturing execution (MES) functionality?

No. Native B1 includes only a basic Production module (BOM, work orders, backflushing). Real shop-floor execution and finite scheduling require Beas Manufacturing, a separate add-on with its own license cost.

### Why is SAP Business One implementation so expensive?

Implementation is billed by the VAR for discovery, configuration, data migration, and training, and the scope — and therefore the price — varies significantly by reseller and by how much customization the shop needs. There's no fixed implementation fee across the SAP B1 ecosystem.
